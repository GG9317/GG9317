# 3. Build Something That Sells While You Sleep

| Stage | What to do | Done when | #1 mistake → how to avoid it |
|-------|-----------|-----------|------------------------------|
| **1. Demand research** | Look for *existing spending*: best-sellers on Gumroad/Etsy/Amazon/Udemy in your niche, 1–3★ reviews (the gaps), Reddit/forum threads asking "is there a tool/template for…". | 3+ competitors making sales, plus 10+ written complaints/requests you could solve. | Treating "no competitors" as an opportunity. It usually means no market. **Competition is proof of demand.** |
| **2. Validation** | Write a sales page *before* building it. Drive 100–300 targeted visitors (communities, DMs to your network, a small ad test). Take pre-orders or a waitlist with a deposit. | ≥5 paid pre-orders (or ≥10% waitlist signup from cold traffic). | Counting compliments and likes as validation. **Only money or a hard commitment counts.** |
| **3. Creation** | Build the smallest version that delivers the promised result. Timebox it to 2–4 weeks. | It produces the core outcome for a pre-order buyer. | Perfectionism. **"Done" = solves the problem. "Finished" doesn't exist.** Ship v1 and improve with buyers. |
| **4. Pricing** | Price on the value of the outcome, not hours spent. Offer 3 tiers (core / + extras / + done-with-you or bundle). | Middle tier is the most picked. | Underpricing ($5–9) to "get sales". It attracts low-commitment buyers and leaves no margin for ads or affiliates. |
| **5. Launch** | Pre-order buyers → network → 3–5 communities (value-first posts) → partners/affiliates at 30–50% commission → a Product Hunt/marketplace listing. | First 10 sales from strangers. | Launching to silence. **Build a waitlist during creation**, even if it's 30 people. |
| **6. Evergreen marketing** | SEO articles/videos around problem keywords, a lead magnet into the email sequence, marketplace listings, affiliates. | Sales happen in weeks with no posting. | Relying only on launch spikes. |
| **7. Improvement loop** | Survey every buyer at day 7 ("What almost stopped you? What's missing?"). Ship updates, raise the price with each major version. | Refund rate < 5%, plus testimonials coming in. | Ignoring silent churn and refunds. They are the most honest feedback you get. |
| **8. Expansion** | Bundle → advanced version → companion product → subscription/updates → course teaching the method. | Second product sells to the first product's buyers. | Building product #2 for a *new* audience. **Sell more to the same buyer first.** |

## Longevity filter (still earning in 3 years?)
Choose problems tied to durable needs: money, careers, health, business operations, core professional tools.
Avoid products whose value depends on one platform's current quirk or one trend.
*Assumption to watch:* AI tools may commoditize generic templates and info products. Specificity, curation, and trust are the defense.
